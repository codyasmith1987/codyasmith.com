# Utah enforceability research for standard-v7 clauses (2026-06-04)

Legal RESEARCH (public record), not legal advice. Done at the attorney's advisory (research instead of
retainer). Governing law Utah; venue Iron County; all clients B2B. Backstop: attorney spot-checks cites.
The B2B posture does real work throughout — it strips Utah's consumer overlays (Title 70C, auto-renewal,
57-22-4, Consumer Sales Practices Act) and makes unconscionability hard. Keep every client a business.

## 1. No-proration / full-month-owed — ENFORCEABLE AS DRAFTED (reframe to bulletproof)
Utah treats liquidated-damages like any other contract term, NOT heightened penalty scrutiny:
*Commercial Real Estate Investment, L.C. v. Comcast of Utah II, Inc.*, 2012 UT 49, 285 P.3d 1193
(https://law.justia.com/cases/utah/supreme-court/2012/20090847.html). Unconscionability backstop:
*Sosa v. Paulos*, 924 P.2d 357 (Utah 1996). UCC penalty statute (70A-2-718) does NOT apply (goods, not
services). REFRAME: frame the fee as price EARNED for RESERVED CAPACITY, not breach damages; avoid the
words penalty/damages/forfeiture; do NOT stack full-month with acceleration of future months.
Cite caveat: full Comcast opinion not directly fetchable (403); holding corroborated across sources.

## 2. 1.5%/mo (18%/yr) interest + collection/attorneys' fees — ENFORCEABLE (minor wording)
No Utah commercial usury cap: Utah Code 15-1-1(1) — parties "may agree upon any rate of interest for the
contract, including a contract for services" (https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title15/Chapter1/15-1-S1.html).
Consumer caps (Title 70C; 57-22-4) exempt B2B by their terms. Attorneys' fees: Utah Code 78B-5-826 makes
any one-way fee clause RECIPROCAL by operation of law — a prevailing CLIENT can recover fees from the
Practice; cannot be drafted around (https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title78B/Chapter5/78B-5-S826.html).
Reasonableness ceiling: *Dixie State Bank v. Bracken*, 764 P.2d 985, 990 (Utah 1988). NOTE: v7 s23 is
ALREADY reciprocal ("prevailing Party... reasonable attorneys' fees and costs") — good, no one-way fix
needed. WORDING: state "1.5% per month (18% per annum)" on "past due" amounts, accruing from invoice
date; keep "reasonable costs of collection." Business reality for Cody: if he sues and loses, the client
recovers ITS fees from him — price that into any decision to litigate.
Cite caveat: Bracken "fee cannot exceed the fee agreement" sub-rule + pre-suit collection-costs category
are the softest points (no verbatim primary / no on-point case).

## 3. Suspension that takes the site offline — ENFORCEABLE (behavioral guardrails required)
First-material-breach doctrine: *CCD, L.C. v. Millsap*, 2005 UT 42 — a party first in material breach
"cannot complain if the other party thereafter refuses to perform" (https://cases.justia.com/utah/supreme-court/CCD070805.pdf).
Good-faith covenant can't override express terms: *Vander Veur v. Groove Entertainment Techs.*, 2019 UT 64
P10. Gross negligence (the s12 carve-out) CANNOT be disclaimed: *Berry v. Greater Park City Co.*, 2007 UT
87, 171 P.3d 442, 449; *Daniels v. Gamma West*, 221 P.3d 256, 269 (Utah 2009). So the defense is
BEHAVIORAL, in the wording + in practice: (1) notice + cure before suspension; (2) trigger only on
past-due AND UNDISPUTED amounts; (3) restate downtime as an expressly bargained, foreseen consequence of
non-payment, not a Practice breach; (4) covenant to PRESERVE data, no irreversible acts (no domain
cancellation/transfer, no deletion); (5) "good faith and commercially reasonable" recital. Ties to Cody's
"shutoffs always manual" decision — manual = careful = defensible.

## 4. Credential/account possessory HOLD — NOT ENFORCEABLE as a hard hold; REWRITE (the standout risk)
Conversion: *Fibro Trust, Inc. v. Brahman Financial, Inc.*, 1999 UT 13, 974 P.2d 288 (willful
interference depriving the owner of use/possession; owner has immediate right to possession). Good-faith
belief is NO defense: *Allred v. Hinkley*, 328 P.2d 726, 728 (Utah 1958). No statutory lien reaches
digital credentials (repairman's lien 38-2-3 = tangible only; intangible-reaching lien is attorney-only,
38-2-7 — negative implication cuts against a vendor). REQUIRED REWRITE: strike any "we hold your logins
until you pay." Replace with (a) affirm Client owns/retains its own accounts/credentials/domains and the
Practice will not suspend/alter/lock/transfer/impair them as leverage; (b) the Practice may delay its OWN
offboarding deliverables (its handover document, paid-for deliverables, migration help) until the account
is paid current. Never change passwords, remove the Client's access, or redirect/transfer a domain to
compel payment. GENUINELY UNSETTLED in Utah (no on-point case on pure digital credentials); the
conservative rewrite is safe but a licensed attorney SHOULD bless it. [LAWYER - the one that most needs it]

## 5. Cumulative/independent remedies + concurrent cure — ENFORCEABLE AS DRAFTED (belt-and-suspenders)
Utah favors it. Election of remedies is narrow (anti-double-recovery only, binds only "once one remedy is
pursued to a determinative conclusion"), with a "cruel dilemma" safety valve against forced early
elections: *Helf v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc.*, 2015 UT 81, 361 P.3d 63, PP68-86 (verified via *Cohen Braffits*
2024 UT App 12 + *H&P Investments v. iLux* 2021 UT App 113, which quote it with matching pincites).
Non-exclusivity default (persuasive, goods): 70A-2-719(1)(b). WORDING: add an express
non-election / cumulative / non-tolling clause + "nothing entitles the Practice to a double recovery of
money damages for the same unpaid amount" (keeps it inside Helf's only real limit).
Cite caveat: Helf + Resource Management v. Weston Ranch (706 P.2d 1028) not directly fetched (403).
