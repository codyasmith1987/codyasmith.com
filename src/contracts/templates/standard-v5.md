## 2. The engagement, in plain language

The Practice sells three products. The Client may buy one, two, or all three under this agreement. The products are sold separately and never bundled into a single combined fee. Each is described in the corresponding section below. Specific product selections, tiers, pricing, and scope are set in **Schedule A** attached to this agreement and incorporated by reference.

The relationship runs like a gym membership. The Client pays an agreed monthly fee. The Practice keeps things stable, answers questions, and does work against an agreed pool of hours. Some months the Client uses every included hour; most months the Client does not. Either way the monthly fee is the price of the seat at the table. Unused included hours do not roll over.

## 3. The three products

### 3.1 Web Management

Web Management keeps an existing website running, secure, monitored, updated, and backed up for a fixed monthly fee. It is sold per site. Each tier includes hosting, daily backups, security monitoring, uptime monitoring, software updates at the cadence stated in the tier, and a pool of hands-on hours. Specific tier, included hours, response time, and per-site count are set in Schedule A.

When the Client has more than one site under Web Management, each managed site is routed to its own ecosystem (A, B, or C) by its own page count, and the engagement's chosen tier (Good, Better, or Best) is applied to every site. The primary site is priced at the full base monthly and full base onboarding for its routed ecosystem at the chosen tier. Each additional managed site is priced at eighty percent (80%) of the base monthly **for that site's own ecosystem at the chosen tier**, and at eighty percent (80%) of the base onboarding **for that site's own ecosystem at the chosen tier**. The multi-site discount applies linearly across sites; it does not compound. Pooled hours scale proportionally with each additional site. The specific tier, the per-site ecosystem assignment, and the resulting monthly and onboarding amounts for each managed site are listed individually in Schedule A.

Web Management does not include marketing strategy, content production at scale, paid media management, advertising creative, hardware purchases, or anything else carved out in Schedule A's exclusions.

### 3.2 Marketing Consulting

Marketing Consulting is **strategic advisory** applied to the Client's business. It covers whatever the Client raises that benefits from outside thinking, which in practice has included marketing, search and content strategy, brand positioning, operations, hiring guidance, vendor selection, organizational design, and growth planning. The product is named "Marketing Consulting" for brand continuity; the scope is broader-advisory.

The product is **advice only, never execution**. The operational test is simple:

| Advisory (in scope) | Execution (not in scope) |
| --- | --- |
| The Practice's mind producing the recommendation | The Practice's hands producing the artifact |
| Strategy calls, written advisories, reviews, recommendation memos | Writing copy, building pages, setting up campaigns, updating tags on a site |
| Reviewing a Client draft and marking it up | Drafting the next version from scratch |
| Hiring guidance and org-structure thinking | Recruiting, interviewing, onboarding hires |
| Vendor selection advice | Contracting and managing the vendor on the Client's behalf |
| Operational consulting (workflows, tooling decisions, process design) | Building the workflow, installing the tooling, executing the process |

Execution does not become consulting through a change order. If the Client wants the Practice to **do** the recommended work, that routes to one of three places: (a) Web Management hours, when the work fits an existing managed site and the pool has room; (b) Web Management overage, with the Client's written approval per section 5.4; (c) a separate Build Statement of Work for net-new builds or implementation projects; or (d) a separate Statement of Work for non-web execution such as copy production at scale, campaign setup, social or ad creative, research production, or similar. The Marketing Consulting product itself never executes.

Marketing Consulting is sold once for the whole business, regardless of how many sites or entities the Client operates. The tier set in Schedule A defines the strategic cycle and the cap on what is included. Specifically the monthly fee buys attention and thinking, not unlimited production:

- The number of strategy calls set in Schedule A for the chosen tier (for example, one 30-minute call per month at Better; one 60-minute call per month at Best).
- The number of deep advisories per cycle set in Schedule A (for example, one or two per quarter at Better; one per month at Best).
- Performance reporting at the cadence set in Schedule A.

A request that exceeds these limits is new work. The Practice quotes it under the change-order mechanism in section 8.

### 3.3 Build work

Build work is the design and implementation of a new website, new feature, or new system. It is not part of Web Management or Marketing Consulting. Each build is separately scoped and priced as a fixed-fee project in a Build Statement of Work that states the scope, deliverables, payment schedule, launch criteria, and timeline. Build payment is one hundred percent (100%) at signing unless the Statement of Work states otherwise. The build replaces the onboarding fee for the site it produces.

If a Statement of Work stages any portion of the build fee to launch rather than collecting it at signing, that staged portion becomes due on the date the Practice notifies the Client in writing that the build is ready for launch. If the Client delays launch by Client decision, withheld content or approval, withheld access, or other Client-side delay for more than ten (10) business days after that notice, the staged portion is due on the eleventh business day regardless of whether launch has occurred. This avoids the Practice carrying a completed-and-paused build indefinitely.

Build work is never absorbed into Web Management hours and is never paid by the monthly fee.

## 4. Term and renewal

This agreement begins on the Effective Date stated on the signature page and continues month-to-month. Either Party may terminate without cause on thirty (30) days' written notice to the other. Specific termination consequences and the offboarding procedure are stated in section 17.

The Client may pause one or more products under this agreement only with the Practice's written agreement. A paused product is not a terminated product and may resume on terms agreed at the time of pause.

## 5. Fees and payment

### 5.1 Fees

All fees, tiers, ecosystem placements, included hours, per-site amounts, overage rates, rush rates, and emergency rates are set in Schedule A. Schedule A is the controlling rate sheet for this agreement.

### 5.2 At signing

Everything due at signing is itemized in Schedule A and includes, at minimum, the first month of every recurring fee under this agreement and the onboarding, audit, or build fee for every product purchased. All amounts due at signing must clear before the Practice begins any work, with one narrow exception: the Practice may, at its sole discretion, begin Web Management security and monitoring functions before signing-day payment clears if the Client's site is at active risk and waiting is unsafe. Every other work category waits for cleared funds.

### 5.3 Recurring billing

Recurring fees are billed monthly, prospectively, covering the coming month of service. Invoices are issued approximately seven (7) days before the service period begins and are due on receipt. Late-payment provisions in section 5.8 begin to apply seven (7) days after the invoice date unless Schedule A states otherwise. Invoices are paid by ACH or credit card on file. Paper check is accepted only with the Practice's written approval. Each invoice carries a single numbering scheme and a verified billing period.

### 5.4 Hours and overage

The monthly fee for Web Management includes the pooled hours stated for the chosen tier in Schedule A. Pooled hours apply across every site the Practice manages for the Client. **Unused included hours do not roll over, accrue, convert to credits, reduce future invoices, or survive termination unless Schedule A expressly says otherwise.**

**Work that is not billable.** Routine contracted management work, including software updates at the contracted cadence, daily backups, security monitoring, uptime monitoring, and the other items defined as "included management" in Schedule A, is not billable. The Practice tracks these hours for its own visibility but does not draw them from the included pool or invoice the Client for them. Routine security under this section means baseline monitoring and contracted updates, not emergency response to a breach, incident, or outage, which is governed by section 5.5.

**Work that draws from the included pool.** Every other Practice-performed task that is not "included management" and not separately scoped Build work. When the included pool runs low for the month, the Practice notifies the Client.

**Pre-approved overage buffer.** The Client pre-approves up to two (2) overage hours per month for time-sensitive work necessary to complete an active request, prevent delay, or avoid operational risk. The Practice exercises this buffer only when notifying the Client first would itself cause the delay or risk it is trying to prevent. The buffer is itemized on the next invoice. The buffer is not cumulative; unused buffer hours do not roll over.

**Overage beyond the buffer.** Requires the Client's prior written approval. Without that approval the work waits until the pool refreshes at the start of the next billing cycle. The overage rate is one hundred dollars ($100) per hour. Billable work past the buffer is never performed unbilled.

### 5.5 Rush and emergency response

Work classified as rush or emergency is billed at the rates in Schedule A:

- **Rush:** same-day response outside contracted response times for the Client's tier. One hundred fifty dollars ($150) per hour, against a two-hour minimum.
- **Emergency:** recovery from a security incident, breach, outage, data loss, malware infection, or other event requiring the Practice to act outside scheduled work. Two hundred dollars ($200) per hour, against a two-hour minimum.

Rush and emergency hours are billed against the next invoice, not drawn from the included pool or the pre-approved buffer in section 5.4. Emergency response is billable even where the emergency relates to security or stability, except where the event is caused by the Practice's own breach of this agreement.

### 5.6 Pass-through costs

Plugin and software licenses, subscription costs, paid connectors, hosting beyond the agreed scope, domain renewals, stock assets, and any other third-party expense incurred for the Client's benefit are pass-through costs. They are billed at the Practice's actual cost, with no markup. Annual licenses are billed at full annual cost on the first invoice for that license, not spread monthly, because the Client may terminate at any time and the Practice will not hold an annual bill for a terminated service. A license shared across multiple of the Practice's clients is split proportionally to estimated usage or by another reasonable allocation method documented to the Client at the time of billing. The Client is billed only its allocated share.

A pass-through cost is not incurred without the Client's prior written approval, except for items defined as standard for the Client's tier in Schedule A (for example, the $15-per-site monthly plugin management fee).

**Pass-through cost changes.** When a vendor changes its pricing for a pass-through item under this agreement, the new price flows through to the Client at the Practice's actual cost on the next invoice after the change takes effect, with reasonable documentation provided on request. Vendor-driven changes do not require the thirty (30) day notice in section 5.7.

### 5.7 Practice-controlled pricing changes

The Practice will not change its own fees, tier inclusions, included hours, overage rate, rush rate, or emergency rate without thirty (30) days' written notice to the Client. Notice given mid-cycle takes effect at the next billing cycle boundary. A price is never reduced without a matching, visible reduction in scope, except where Schedule A or a signed amendment expressly provides otherwise.

### 5.8 Late payment and suspension

If an invoice is not paid within seven (7) days of the invoice date, the unpaid balance accrues interest at a rate of one and one-half percent (1.5%) per month or the maximum rate allowed under Utah law, whichever is less, plus the Practice's reasonable costs of collection.

Late-payment suspension is split by criticality of the work:

- **Non-critical work** (strategy calls, advisories, new requests, change-order work, marketing consulting deliverables, content updates, optimization work, and any work that is not actively keeping a managed site running). The Practice may pause this work when an invoice is more than seven (7) days past due, on written notice to the Client.
- **Critical continuity work** (hosting, daily backups, security monitoring, uptime monitoring, software updates at the contracted cadence). The Practice continues this work until an invoice is more than thirty (30) days past due, after which the Practice may suspend the affected product on ten (10) days' additional written notice and cure period.

Suspension is not termination. Resumption requires payment of the past-due balance and any reasonable resumption fee stated in Schedule A.

### 5.9 Tax

To the Practice's understanding under current Utah law, the Practice's consulting and management services are not subject to Utah sales tax. Reimbursed pass-through software costs are billed at the Practice's cost. The Parties acknowledge that tax treatment of pass-through software and similar costs depends on factors not addressed in this agreement; this agreement does not assert which Party bears that tax.

The Practice will separately state taxable and nontaxable charges where required by law, and will pass through any legally required sales tax, use tax, or similar tax on services or pass-through costs to the Client. If applicable tax law changes, the Practice will give written notice and pass any required tax through to the Client.

The Client receives a Form W-9 from the Practice at signing.

## 6. Onboarding and takeover

### 6.1 The onboarding fee, and what it pays for

The onboarding or takeover fee in Schedule A pays for the heavy first month of every engagement. Onboarding is expected to be more time-intensive than every month after it; that is by design and that is what the fee is for. Onboarding ends on a date: the close of month one of the relevant product. It does not end "when a task list is empty." After that date the engagement settles into maintenance, requests, and advice at the agreed tier.

### 6.2 The takeover audit

Every site the Practice takes over from a prior vendor receives a takeover audit before significant other work begins. The audit establishes, at minimum, who owns and controls every account associated with the Client's web presence, including the domain registrar, DNS provider, email and email authentication records, hosting, plugin and software licenses, analytics, advertising accounts, customer relationship management connections, payment processor, content delivery network, and any third-party service tied to the site.

The audit also records what the Client and any prior vendor actually supplied, and when. Where an account or license can only be logged on the Client's word, the entry is marked as the Client's representation, not as verified fact. A representation later found to be incomplete or incorrect is the Client's gap, not the Practice's error, and additional work made necessary by that gap is billable.

### 6.3 Nameservers and DNS

The Practice does not change a nameserver, DNS record, or email authentication record (SPF, DKIM, DMARC, MX, or equivalent) without first identifying who controls the domain and the email, coordinating the change with that party in writing, and confirming what will change and when. This requirement is mandatory.

### 6.4 First-month communication

Communication during the first month of every product is heavier than communication after it, and the heavier communication is framed in writing as part of what onboarding covers. After month one, contact settles into maintenance, requests, and advice. The Practice does not promise constant contact as a steady state.

## 7. Client access, credentials, and obligations

### 7.1 Day-one access

The Client provides administrator-level access to every system the Practice needs to deliver the services purchased, on the Effective Date of this agreement. The required access list is itemized in Schedule A and includes the domain registrar, DNS provider, email host and email authentication tools, web hosting account, content management system, analytics tools, advertising accounts where applicable, form tools, customer relationship management, payment processor where applicable, and any third-party service tied to the site or to the work in scope.

Access is a named, dated obligation of the Client.

### 7.2 Consequence when access is withheld or delayed

If access required under section 7.1 is not delivered by the date stated in Schedule A, the Practice will, within five (5) business days, notify the Client in writing identifying what is missing. The Client has ten (10) business days from that notice to cure. During the cure period, the Practice's clock for any contracted deliverable that depends on the missing access is paused, and no Practice delay caused by missing access is a Practice breach.

If the missing access is not cured within thirty (30) days of the Practice's notice, the Practice may, at its option, suspend the affected product, terminate this agreement under section 17, or charge the additional Practice time spent working around the missing access as overage hours under section 5.4.

### 7.3 Credentials are the Client's property

Every credential, account, license, and platform connection used by the Practice on the Client's behalf remains the Client's property. New accounts opened during the engagement are opened in the Client's name wherever practical. When this agreement ends, the Client leaves with every credential, every account, every license, and every connection associated with the Client's business, and the Practice removes its own access from those accounts.

The Practice does not transfer or attempt to transfer ownership of a Client account or license to itself or to any third party at any time during or after the engagement.

### 7.4 Storage of Client credentials by the Practice

The Practice stores Client credentials only in a designated secure credential store maintained by the Practice. The Practice does not paste credentials into general-purpose chats, documents, or email except in the moment of a documented operational need, after which the credential is removed from that location. The Client may ask which credential store is in use and what its security posture is; the Practice will answer in writing.

### 7.5 Client decisions

The Client commits to responding to written requests for decisions from the Practice within five (5) business days, unless the request is itself flagged by the Practice as low-priority. Work that depends on an outstanding decision does not start. The Practice is not required to make repeated follow-up requests for the same outstanding decision after the initial written request is sent, and does not perform unapproved work on the Client's behalf.

The Practice tracks every decision raised with the Client. A decision the Client does not make is not a Practice failure. The Practice records what was raised, when, and the Client's response (including non-response) so the matter can be revisited if the Client returns to it later.

### 7.6 When the Client overrides a Practice recommendation

The Client owns its own systems and its own choices. The Practice gives its recommendation once, in writing, and does not run real-time intervention to stop a Client decision the Practice would not advise. A recommendation the Client overrides is recorded once and not relitigated.

If the Client retains a third-party vendor, employee, or contractor against the Practice's recommendation, the Practice is not responsible for the consequences of that retention. Repairing damage caused by a third-party vendor or by Client-team action is billable work, scoped and quoted like any other change.

This section applies to decisions within the Client's own systems and authority. It does not extend to imminent damage to the Practice, its intellectual property, or a third party.

## 8. Change orders

Any work outside the scope, included hours, pre-approved buffer, or tier inclusions set in Schedule A is new work and requires a written change order. A change order states what is being added, the fixed fee or estimated hours at the agreed rate, the deliverables, and the impact on timeline. Work under a change order does not start until the Client approves the change order in writing.

A change order is not a renegotiation of this agreement. Both Parties' obligations under this agreement continue unchanged outside the scope of the change order. Change orders do not convert Marketing Consulting into execution; execution routes through Web Management hours, a Build Statement of Work, or a separate Statement of Work per section 3.2.

"Confidential Information" means non-public business and technical information of a Party, including credentials, customer lists, internal financials, product roadmaps, source code, and any information identified in writing as confidential. Confidential Information does not include information that (i) is or becomes public through no fault of the receiving Party, (ii) the receiving Party can show was rightfully in its possession before disclosure, (iii) is independently developed by the receiving Party without use of the disclosing Party's Confidential Information, or (iv) is rightfully received from a third party without confidentiality obligations.

## 17. Termination and offboarding

### 17.1 Termination for convenience

Either Party may terminate this agreement, or any individual product under it, without cause on thirty (30) days' written notice to the other.

### 17.2 Termination for cause

Either Party may terminate this agreement immediately on written notice if the other Party (a) materially breaches this agreement and fails to cure the breach within fifteen (15) days of written notice, (b) becomes insolvent, makes an assignment for the benefit of creditors, or is the subject of any bankruptcy proceeding that is not dismissed within sixty (60) days, or (c) ceases to conduct business in the ordinary course.

A material breach by the Client includes, without limitation, non-payment of any amount due under section 5 that remains uncured under section 5.8.

### 17.3 Effect of termination

On the effective date of termination, the Client pays all amounts due through that date.

Monthly recurring fees are not refunded once a billing period has begun. Any monthly recurring fee with a billing period that begins during the notice period is invoiced in full and is non-refundable. The Client also pays earned but uninvoiced overage hours, rush and emergency hours, pass-through costs, and any approved change-order or Build Statement of Work amounts in progress.

The Practice delivers to the Client every credential, account ownership, license assignment, and platform connection associated with the Client's business that the Practice holds on the Client's behalf, in a written handover document.

The Practice removes its own administrator-level access from the Client's accounts within seven (7) business days of the effective date of termination.

The Practice delivers to the Client any paid-for Deliverables not yet handed over, in a usable form, within thirty (30) days of the effective date of termination. Drafts, working files, and Practice IP are not delivered.

Each Party returns or destroys the other Party's Confidential Information, except copies retained for legal, regulatory, or backup-system retention purposes, which remain subject to section 9 for as long as they are retained.
